\title{The Thurstone Mirror Map:\\ Fast Inversion, Generalized Entropy\\ and Multiway Choice Geometry}
\author{Peter Cotton}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The map from latent abilities to winning probabilities in a multi-entrant
Thurstone contest is a convex gradient map whose Jacobian, for independent
translated noise, is a weighted complete-graph Laplacian.  We exploit this
organizing fact in three directions.  First, we assemble a global inverse
theorem: under full-support noise the map is a diffeomorphism from the
translation-quotient of ability space onto the open probability simplex, with
injectivity and surjectivity due in essence to prior work in discrete-choice
econometrics; our contribution is the quantitative side---the explicit
Laplacian weights, inverse conditioning governed by the spectral gap, a
matrix-tree formula for the Jacobian determinant, and an exact description of
degeneracy at the simplex boundary.  Second, the convex conjugate of the
expected-winner surplus is a generalized entropy whose gradient is the inverse
map, placing the ``ability transform'' in the family of mirror maps: softmax
and Shannon entropy are the special case of Gumbel noise.  Third, and mainly,
we show the Laplacian need never be formed: a Hessian-vector product costs
$O(nM)$ on an $M$-point lattice via shared hazard aggregates, enabling exact
joint Newton--conjugate-gradient inversion of an entire field at near-linear
cost.  We give the algorithm in complete detail, including an exact repair
scheme that keeps the fast product correct for atoms, truncated supports and
other degenerate fields, and a renormalization identity showing that matching
probability \emph{ratios} costs nothing: the rescaled residual has Jacobian
exactly equal to the negative Laplacian.  Numerical experiments confirm
machine-precision agreement with closed forms, superlinear Newton convergence,
and three-orders-of-magnitude speedups over dense Jacobian assembly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fix $n$ contestants with latent abilities $a = (a_1,\dots,a_n) \in \R^n$ and
independent performances
\begin{equation}
  X_i = a_i + \varepsilon_i,
  \qquad \varepsilon_i \sim F \text{ i.i.d.},
  \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where the largest performance wins.\footnote{Racing applications use the
mirror convention in which the \emph{smallest} performance (a time) wins; the
two are exchanged by $a \mapsto -a$ and all results below carry over with one
sign flip, made explicit in Appendix~\ref{app:degenerate}.}  The winning
probabilities
\begin{equation}
  p_i(a) = \Pr\Bigl(X_i = \max_j X_j\Bigr)
  \label{eq:pmap}
\end{equation}
define a map that is invariant under common translation, so the natural
domain is the quotient $\R^n / \langle \one \rangle$, which we identify with
the mean-zero subspace $\perpone$:
\[
  T : \perpone \longrightarrow \intsimplex, \qquad a \mapsto p(a).
\]
This is the multiway generalization of \textcite{thurstone1927}; the lattice
algorithms of \textcite{cotton2021} compute $T$ and invert it numerically at
$O(n)$ cost per field.  The present paper is about the mathematical structure
of $T$ and how that structure yields a near-linear \emph{joint} inversion
algorithm with provable behaviour in every degenerate configuration.

Our starting point is classical.  The expected winning performance, or social
surplus,
\begin{equation}
  \Psi(a) = \mathbb{E}\Bigl[\max_i\, (a_i + \varepsilon_i)\Bigr]
  \label{eq:surplus}
\end{equation}
is convex, and under mild continuity conditions $\nabla \Psi = p$: choice
probabilities are the gradient of the surplus.  This is the
Williams--Daly--Zachary theorem of additive random-utility theory
\parencite{williams1977,daly1978,mcfadden1981}.  What the general theory does
not provide is the \emph{form} of the second derivative.  For independent
translated noise it is strikingly simple (Theorem~\ref{thm:laplacian}): with
common density $f$ and CDF $F$,
\[
  D T(a) = \nabla^2 \Psi(a) = L(w(a)),
\]
a weighted complete-graph Laplacian with explicit positive weights
$w_{ij}(a) = \int f(x-a_i) f(x-a_j) \prod_{k \ne i,j} F(x-a_k) \dd x$.
Almost everything else in the paper flows from this fact:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Geometry} (\S\ref{sec:laplacian}--\S\ref{sec:diffeo}): rank
    $n-1$ with kernel $\langle\one\rangle$, strict monotonicity on
    $\perpone$, a matrix-tree formula for the Jacobian determinant, inverse
    sensitivity controlled by $1/\lambda_2(L)$, and---combined with
    injectivity and surjectivity results from the econometrics
    literature---a global diffeomorphism theorem
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:diffeo}).
  \item \textbf{Duality} (\S\ref{sec:entropy}): the convex conjugate
    $\Omega = \Psi^*$ restricted to the simplex is a generalized entropy
    with $T^{-1} = \nabla \Omega$; the ability transform is a non-logit
    mirror map, softmax/Shannon being the Gumbel special case.
  \item \textbf{Computation} (\S\ref{sec:algorithm}, Appendices
    \ref{app:lattice}--\ref{app:newton}): on an $M$-point lattice a
    Hessian-vector product costs $O(nM)$ through shared hazard aggregates
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:hvp}), so an exact joint Newton step---all
    contestants moving together, every cross-effect included---costs a few
    matrix-free conjugate-gradient iterations rather than an
    $O(n^2 M)$ Jacobian assembly.  A renormalization identity
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:renorm}) shows that matching a target's probability
    \emph{ratios}, the natural criterion for market data whose quoted
    prices sum to one, changes the Jacobian not at all: $\one^\top L = 0$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What is not new.}
We are careful about attribution.  The gradient representation
$\nabla\Psi = p$ is classical \parencite{mcfadden1981}.  Injectivity of
choice-probability maps is the Hotz--Miller inversion
\parencite{hotzmiller1993}, in full generality the connected-substitutes
theorem of \textcite{berrygandhihaile2013}.  Surjectivity onto the open
simplex under full-support noise is due to
\textcite{noretstakahashi2013}.  The convex-conjugate view of the inversion,
including its computational use, appears in
\textcite{chiong2016}, and ``generalized entropy'' in the sense used here is
developed by \textcite{fosgerau2020}.  The qualitative diffeomorphism
statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:diffeo} is therefore an assembly of known
pieces, recorded here because we need its quantitative refinements.  The
explicit Laplacian weight formula, the conditioning and boundary analysis,
the $O(nM)$ Hessian-vector product with its exact repair scheme, and the
ratio-matching Newton--CG solver are, to our knowledge, new.

\section{The Thurstone map and its gradient structure}
\label{sec:setup}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:noise}
The noise CDF $F$ is absolutely continuous with density $f$ that is bounded,
continuous, and strictly positive on $\R$ (full support), and
$\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon| < \infty$.
\end{assumption}

Full support guarantees $p(a) \in \intsimplex$ for every finite $a$ and rules
out ties, so \eqref{eq:pmap} is unambiguous.  Conditioning on $X_i = x$
gives the standard representation
\begin{equation}
  p_i(a) = \int_{\R} f(x - a_i) \prod_{k \ne i} F(x - a_k) \dd x .
  \label{eq:pint}
\end{equation}
Translation invariance $p(a + b\one) = p(a)$ is immediate.  The surplus
\eqref{eq:surplus} is finite by integrability, convex as an expectation of a
maximum of affine functions, and satisfies $\Psi(a+b\one) = \Psi(a) + b$; the
Williams--Daly--Zachary theorem gives
\begin{equation}
  \nabla \Psi(a) = p(a).
  \label{eq:wdz}
\end{equation}

\section{The Laplacian Jacobian}
\label{sec:laplacian}

For a symmetric nonnegative weight matrix $W = (w_{ij})$ with zero diagonal,
write $L(W) = \diag(W\one) - W$ for the graph Laplacian.

\begin{theorem}[Laplacian Jacobian]
\label{thm:laplacian}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:noise}, $T$ is continuously differentiable and
\[
  D T(a) = \nabla^2 \Psi(a) = L\bigl(w(a)\bigr),
  \qquad
  w_{ij}(a) = \int_{\R} f(x-a_i)\, f(x-a_j) \prod_{k \ne i,j} F(x-a_k) \dd x .
\]
All off-diagonal weights are strictly positive, i.e.\ the interaction graph is
complete.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $i \ne j$ and differentiate \eqref{eq:pint} in $a_j$.  The integrand is
dominated by $f(x-a_i)$ uniformly for $a_j$ in a neighbourhood, and
$\partial_{a_j} F(x-a_j) = -f(x-a_j)$, so differentiation under the integral
sign (justified by dominated convergence and continuity of $f$) gives
$\partial p_i / \partial a_j = -w_{ij}(a) < 0$, with $w_{ij}$ as displayed and
strictly positive because $f > 0$ everywhere and $F > 0$ on a half-line.
Symmetry $w_{ij} = w_{ji}$ is manifest.  Differentiating the identity
$p_i(a + b\one) = p_i(a)$ in $b$ yields
$\sum_j \partial p_i/\partial a_j = 0$, hence
$\partial p_i / \partial a_i = \sum_{j\ne i} w_{ij}$.  Assembling,
$DT = \diag(w\one) - w = L(w)$.  Continuity of $a \mapsto w(a)$ follows from
dominated convergence again, giving $C^1$.  Finally
$DT = D(\nabla\Psi) = \nabla^2\Psi$ by \eqref{eq:wdz}.
\end{proof}

The immediate consequences justify calling this an organizing fact.

\begin{proposition}[Structure]
\label{prop:structure}
For every $a$: (i) $L(w(a))$ is symmetric positive semidefinite with
$\ker L = \langle \one \rangle$ and $\rank L = n-1$; (ii) $T$ restricted to
$\perpone$ is strictly monotone:
$(a-b)^\top (T(a) - T(b)) > 0$ for $a \ne b \in \perpone$; (iii) the
determinant of $DT$ restricted to $\perpone$ equals $n$ times the weighted
spanning-tree polynomial of $w(a)$ (matrix-tree theorem); (iv) for
$p^* = T(a^*)$ and a perturbed target $p$ near $p^*$, both in
$\intsimplex$,
\[
  \| T^{-1}(p) - T^{-1}(p^*) \|_2 \;\le\; \frac{\|p - p^*\|_2}{\lambda_2(L(w(a^*)))}
  + o(\|p - p^*\|_2),
\]
where $\lambda_2$ is the smallest nonzero (Fiedler) eigenvalue.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Positive semidefiniteness and the kernel are standard for Laplacians of
connected graphs with positive weights, and the graph here is complete.
(ii) By (i), $\Psi$ restricted to $\perpone$ has positive-definite Hessian,
hence is strictly convex, and gradients of strictly convex functions are
strictly monotone.  (iii) is the weighted matrix-tree theorem applied to any
cofactor of $L$.  (iv) is the inverse-function-theorem bound
$\|DT^{-1}\|_{2 \to 2} = 1/\lambda_2$ on $\perpone$.
\end{proof}

Part (iv) is the quantitative content of the folklore statement that
inverting prices is ill-conditioned near the boundary of the simplex: as any
$p_i \to 0$ or $1$, contestant $i$'s density separates from the field, all
$w_{ij}$ involving $i$ decay (at the rate of the overlap integral, e.g.\
Gaussian-tail in the Gaussian case), and $\lambda_2 \to 0$.  Section
\ref{sec:experiments} reports $1/\lambda_2$ for a realistic field.

\section{The global diffeomorphism}
\label{sec:diffeo}

\begin{theorem}[Global diffeomorphism; assembled]
\label{thm:diffeo}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:noise},
$T : \perpone \to \intsimplex$ is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Local diffeomorphism: $DT$ restricted to $\perpone$ is positive definite by
Theorem~\ref{thm:laplacian} and Proposition~\ref{prop:structure}(i), so the
inverse function theorem applies at every point.  Injectivity: strict
monotonicity, Proposition~\ref{prop:structure}(ii); this is a special case of
the inversions of \textcite{hotzmiller1993} and
\textcite{berrygandhihaile2013}.  Surjectivity onto $\intsimplex$ is
Theorem~1 of \textcite{noretstakahashi2013}, whose hypothesis---additive
shocks with a density---is implied by Assumption~\ref{ass:noise}.  A smooth
bijection with everywhere-invertible differential has smooth inverse.
\end{proof}

We emphasize again that the three ingredients are individually known; the
value here is the package together with the quantitative structure of
\S\ref{sec:laplacian}, which the econometric treatments do not use.

\section{Generalized entropy and the mirror map}
\label{sec:entropy}

Define the convex conjugate of the surplus restricted to the gauge slice,
\[
  \Omega(p) = \sup_{a \in \perpone} \bigl\{ p^\top a - \Psi(a) \bigr\},
  \qquad p \in \intsimplex .
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:diffeo} the supremum is attained at $a = T^{-1}(p)$, and
standard conjugate duality \parencite{rockafellar1970} gives
\begin{equation}
  T^{-1}(p) = \nabla \Omega(p) \big|_{\perpone},
  \qquad
  \nabla^2 \Omega(p) = \bigl( L(w(T^{-1}(p))) \bigr)^{+},
  \label{eq:mirror}
\end{equation}
the pseudoinverse taken on $\perpone$.  For Gumbel noise, $\Psi(a) = \log
\sum_i e^{a_i}$ (up to the Euler constant), $T$ is softmax, and $\Omega(p) =
\sum_i p_i \log p_i$ is negative Shannon entropy: \eqref{eq:mirror} is the
textbook mirror pair.  For Gaussian or arbitrary Thurstone noise, $\Omega$ is
the corresponding \emph{generalized entropy} in the sense of
\textcite{fosgerau2020}, and $T^{-1}$---the ability transform of
\textcite{cotton2021}---is the associated mirror map, computable but not in
closed form.  The dual Hessian in \eqref{eq:mirror} is the pseudoinverse of a
graph Laplacian, i.e.\ a weighted effective-resistance kernel, which is what
makes the conditioning story of Proposition~\ref{prop:structure}(iv) natural:
inverse sensitivity between contestants $i$ and $j$ is their effective
resistance in the interaction graph.  The computational consequence is the
subject of the next section: evaluating the non-logit mirror map at near-linear
cost.  Connections to mirror descent, proper scoring rules and probability
aggregation follow exactly as in the logit case and are not pursued here;
see also \textcite{chiong2016} for the mass-transport formulation.

\section{Near-linear inversion}
\label{sec:algorithm}

We now work on the computational side, where distributions live on a uniform
lattice with $M$ points, spacing $u$, per-contestant probability masses
$f_i(x)$ and survivals $S_i(x)$; heterogeneous shapes and scales are allowed
(the theory above extends mutatis mutandis; independence is what matters).
Full definitions, conventions and edge cases are in
Appendix~\ref{app:lattice}.  Forward pricing---all $n$ win probabilities---
costs $O(nM)$ by the prefix--suffix (``removable field'') construction of
Appendix~\ref{app:lattice}.  The central computational claim is that the
\emph{differential} of the forward map is available at the same cost.

\begin{theorem}[Matrix-free Hessian-vector product]
\label{thm:hvp}
Define the hazards $h_i(x) = f_i(x)/S_i(x)$ where $S_i(x) > 0$, the
leave-one-out masses $q_i(x) = f_i(x)\prod_{k \ne i} S_k(x)$, and the shared
aggregates $H = \sum_j h_j$ and $G_v = \sum_j h_j v_j$.  Wherever all
survivals are positive,
\[
  \bigl(L(w)\,v\bigr)_i \;=\; \sum_{j \ne i} w_{ij}\,(v_i - v_j)
  \;=\; \frac{1}{u}\sum_{x} q_i(x)\,\bigl( v_i H(x) - G_v(x) \bigr),
\]
because the $j = i$ term of the expansion vanishes identically.  All
aggregates are shared across $i$, so the product costs $O(nM)$, versus
$O(n^2 M)$ to form the dense weights.  Points where some $S_j(x) = 0$ while
$f_j(x) > 0$ (atoms, truncated supports) admit an exact division-free repair
at $O(n)$ per such point (Appendix~\ref{app:hvp}), preserving both the total
complexity and exactness for every representable field.
\end{theorem}

The proof, the precise masking threshold, the floating-point error analysis
(hazards are capped so that the subtractive cancellation in $v_iH - G_v$ is
bounded), and pseudocode are in Appendix~\ref{app:hvp}.  With the product in
hand, a Newton step for the inverse problem solves $L\,\delta = r$ by
conjugate gradients restricted to $\perpone$---the natural Krylov space,
since $L$ maps into it---at a handful of $O(nM)$ products.

One further identity removes the last practical obstruction.  Market targets
$\pi$ are quoted normalized, $\sum_i \pi_i = 1$, whereas the lattice's no-tie
forward map sums to one minus a tie mass.  The correct criterion is to match
\emph{ratios}: solve $p(a) = s\,\pi$ with $s = \sum_i p_i(a) / \sum_i \pi_i$
determined by the iterate itself.

\begin{lemma}[Renormalization is free]
\label{lem:renorm}
Let $r(a) = p(a) - s(a)\,\pi$ with $s(a) = \one^\top p(a) / \one^\top \pi$.
Then $\one^\top r(a) = 0$ identically, and
$Dr(a) = \bigl(I - \tfrac{\pi \one^\top}{\one^\top \pi}\bigr) Dp(a) = Dp(a)$:
the renormalization term vanishes because $\one^\top L = 0$.  Newton steps
computed with the unmodified Laplacian are therefore exact for the rescaled
residual.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\one^\top r = \one^\top p - s\,\one^\top\pi = 0$ by the definition of $s$.
Differentiating, $Dr = Dp - \frac{\pi}{\one^\top\pi}\,\one^\top Dp$, and
$\one^\top Dp = \pm\one^\top L = 0$ since $L$ is symmetric with
$L\one = 0$.
\end{proof}

The criterion matters beyond bookkeeping.  An additive matching criterion
(fit $p(a) - \pi$ up to a common constant, the orthogonal projection of the
same infeasibility) silently destroys small probabilities: a uniform offset
of order the tie mass divided by $n$ can absorb a $10^{-6}$ longshot
entirely, and we exhibit exactly this failure in
\S\ref{sec:experiments}.  Ratio matching preserves longshots by
construction.  The complete solver---damped Newton with CG inner loops, a
squared-residual line search, a ridge-regularized fallback direction, ability
clipping to the lattice-representable box with diagnostics, and honest
convergence reporting---is specified line by line in
Appendix~\ref{app:newton}; degenerate fields (zero-mass sentinels,
deterministically dominated contestants, atomic ties) and their exact
semantics are catalogued in Appendix~\ref{app:degenerate}.

\section{Numerical results}
\label{sec:experiments}

All experiments use the open-source \texttt{thurstone} package,\footnote{%
\url{https://github.com/microprediction/thurstone}; the algorithms of this
paper are \texttt{laplacian\_weights}, \texttt{laplacian\_matvec},
\texttt{LaplacianOperator} and \texttt{invert\_outright\_probabilities},
with the test battery in \texttt{tests/test\_laplacian.py} and a runnable
walk-through in \texttt{examples/laplacian\_newton\_demo.py}.}
lattice $M = 801$, spacing $u = 0.05$ unless stated.

\paragraph{Closed-form anchor.}
For two unit-Gaussian contestants at ability gap $\delta$, the weight has the
closed form $w_{12} = e^{-\delta^2/4} / (2\sqrt{\pi})$.  At $\delta = 0.3$
the lattice value agrees to $1.1\times10^{-16}$ at spacing $0.1$,
$1.1\times10^{-16}$ at $0.05$, and $4.4\times10^{-16}$ at $0.025$:
trapezoidal quadrature of smooth rapidly-decaying integrands is spectrally
accurate, so the coarsest practical grid already attains machine precision.
The forward probability converges to $\Phi(\delta/\sqrt2)$ at first order in
$u$ (the excluded lattice tie mass).

\paragraph{Finite-difference convergence.}
Across a battery of smooth fields (heterogeneous scales and skews, extreme
separations, near-ties), central differences of the forward map agree with
the analytic $L(w)$ to relative accuracy $\sim\!3\%$ at $u = 0.05$, and the
gap halves when $u$ halves---first-order convergence, confirming the
analytic Laplacian as the continuum limit of the discrete map's derivative.

\paragraph{Joint Newton--CG inversion.}
A twelve-contestant field with scales drawn from $\{0.7, 1.0, 1.4\}$ and
abilities $\sim \mathcal N(0, 0.8^2)$ has tie mass $0.0315$ and
$\lambda_2 = 0.0092$ (inverse sensitivity $1/\lambda_2 \approx 109$).
Inverting the \emph{normalized} prices recovers all abilities to
$4.4\times10^{-9}$ in eight Newton iterations, with residual history
\[
  9.3\!\times\!10^{-2},\;
  5.0\!\times\!10^{-2},\;
  3.1\!\times\!10^{-3},\;
  5.6\!\times\!10^{-4},\;
  2.5\!\times\!10^{-5},\;
  9.9\!\times\!10^{-7},\;
  3.3\!\times\!10^{-8},\;
  1.1\!\times\!10^{-9},\;
  3.9\!\times\!10^{-11},
\]
superlinear as expected from an exact Jacobian, and the recovered scale
equals the attainable total $0.968471$ to eight digits.  Under the additive
criterion the same solver ``converges'' on a
$(1-2\varepsilon,\,\varepsilon,\,\varepsilon)$ target with
$\varepsilon = 10^{-12}$ while the longshot's achieved probability is
$3\times10^{-3}$---nine orders of magnitude wrong---which is the failure
mode Lemma~\ref{lem:renorm} eliminates.

\paragraph{Robustness.}
The implementation is exact---agreeing with the division-free dense
reference to $10^{-8}$ relative---for every field the lattice can represent,
including atoms, atomic ties, supports truncated at the lattice edge,
zero-mass sentinels and adversarial near-zero survivals; a 25-seed fuzz over
random mixtures of all ingredients passes at that tolerance.  Two degenerate
mechanisms deserve note (details in Appendix~\ref{app:degenerate}): a
deterministically dominated contestant has all incident weights zero and is
an isolated vertex ($\lambda_2 = 0$), and two identical atoms carry the
divergent discrete tie weight $w = 1/u$, the lattice signature of the
continuum map's non-differentiability at an atomic tie.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The Laplacian Jacobian reframes several practical questions.  Calibration
across many overlapping contests, currently done with per-contestant cached
response curves (diagonal Jacobian approximations), can instead take exact
joint Newton steps at near-linear cost; the multidimensional extension---
abilities $a_{ri} = v_r^\top z_i$ projected along contest-specific
directions---separates cleanly into the inversion studied here followed by a
linear or bilinear factorization problem, whose identifiability theory
(contest hypergraph connectivity, gauge groups) we develop elsewhere.

\begin{remark}[Maximally symmetric cube-to-simplex diffeomorphisms]
Composing $T$ with coordinatewise monotone maps produces diffeomorphisms
from the cube $[0,1]^k$ onto the simplex $\Delta^k$ (add one reference
contestant; map each cube coordinate to an ability through a sigmoid), a
construction used in the \texttt{thurstone} package for sampling and
reparametrization.  It is natural to ask for the \emph{most symmetric} such
map.  A canonical answer exists within this paper's theme: take the Brenier
optimal-transport map from the uniform measure on the cube to the uniform
measure on the simplex.  It is the gradient of a convex potential---another
mirror map---pushes volume to volume exactly, and, because the quadratic-cost
transport map between two measures invariant under a common group action is
unique, it is automatically equivariant under the shared symmetry:
permutations of the $k$ cube axes act on the simplex by permuting the $k$
non-reference vertices.  The Thurstone family with identical sigmoid
parameters achieves the equivariance but not exact volume preservation;
quantifying its transport cost against the Brenier optimum, and whether a
noise law exists for which the Thurstone construction \emph{is} optimal,
seems a worthwhile question.  The construction has more freedom than the
equal-parameter family suggests, because mapping $k$ coordinates onto a
$k$-simplex requires \emph{adding} one reference contestant, and nothing
forces the enlarged field to be homogeneous or independent: the reference
contestant's noise scale is a free scalar, and the $k$ coordinate
contestants may be given an exchangeable correlation (say through a common
latent factor) without breaking equivariance, since any symmetric treatment
of the $k$ axes preserves it.  These knobs---reference variance,
exchangeable correlation strength---parametrize a family of equivariant
diffeomorphisms within which one can optimize toward volume uniformity,
plausibly getting much nearer the Brenier map than the independent
homogeneous case allows.  Correlation does not even leave this paper's
framework: for a general additive random-utility field the
Williams--Daly--Zachary conditions still make $DT$ a symmetric
substitutes matrix with zero row sums---a graph Laplacian---so
Proposition~\ref{prop:structure} survives verbatim; what is lost is only
the product form of the weights and with it the $O(nM)$ trick, which is
specific to independence.
\end{remark}

Two further directions are natural.  First, the boundary asymptotics of
$\lambda_2$ deserve sharp constants: the overlap integrals decay at explicit
noise-dependent rates, which would turn
Proposition~\ref{prop:structure}(iv) into a complete uniform conditioning
theory on shrinking neighbourhoods of the boundary.  Second, heteroskedastic
identification---when overlapping contests pin down both location and
scale---inherits the same Laplacian structure blockwise and is the subject
of ongoing work.

\section{Lattice representation and forward pricing}
\label{app:lattice}

\paragraph{Representation.}
A field of $n$ contestants lives on a uniform lattice of $M = 2\ell+1$
points $x_m = m u$, $m = -\ell,\dots,\ell$, spacing $u$.  Contestant $i$ is a
probability mass function $f_i \in \R_{\ge0}^{M}$ with
$\sum_x f_i(x) \in \{0, 1\}$; total mass zero is an explicit sentinel for a
contestant shifted off the lattice.  Masses relate to densities by
$f_i(x) \approx u\,\phi_i(x)$; consequently every quadrature below carries
one factor $1/u$ when it approximates a derivative with respect to physical
ability.  The CDF is the running sum $F_i(x) = \sum_{y \le x} f_i(y)$,
clamped monotone into $[0,1]$, and the survival is
$S_i(x) = 1 - F_i(x) = \Pr(X_i > x)$.  Ability shifts act by fractional
shifting: a shift of $\alpha$ lattice steps blends the CDFs of the two
neighbouring integer shifts with weights $(1-\{\alpha\}, \{\alpha\})$, and
integer shifts beyond the lattice saturate at $\pm(\ell-2)$ steps.  Two
consequences matter later: the forward map is \emph{piecewise linear} in
each ability between lattice multiples (so central finite differences
straddling one step recover the derivative of the underlying smooth map to
$O(u)$), and abilities beyond the saturation box are invisible to the map
(Appendix~\ref{app:newton} clips iterates accordingly).

In this and the next two appendices we use the racing (minimum-wins)
convention of the implementation; Appendix~\ref{app:degenerate} gives the
dictionary to the maximum convention of the main text.

\paragraph{Leave-one-out products.}
The workhorse primitive: given the stacked survival array
$S \in [0,1]^{n \times M}$, compute
$\Lambda_i(x) = \prod_{k \ne i} S_k(x)$ for all $i, x$.  Divide-by-$S_i$ is
forbidden ($S_i$ may be exactly zero); instead form columnwise prefix
products $P_0 = \one$, $P_i = P_{i-1} \odot S_i$ and suffix products
$Q_{n+1} = \one$, $Q_i = Q_{i+1} \odot S_i$, and set
$\Lambda_i = P_{i-1} \odot Q_{i+1}$.  Cost $O(nM)$, exact for arbitrary
zeros.

\paragraph{Forward pricing.}
The no-tie win probability of contestant $i$ (strictly earliest finish) is
\begin{equation}
  p_i = \sum_x f_i(x)\,\Lambda_i(x)
  = \sum_x q_i(x),
  \qquad q_i := f_i \odot \Lambda_i,
  \label{eq:lattice-forward}
\end{equation}
computed for all $i$ simultaneously in $O(nM)$.  On a lattice ties have
positive probability, so $\sum_i p_i = 1 - (\text{tie mass})$; the tie mass
is $O(u)$ for smooth fields and this deficit is exactly what the
renormalization of Lemma~\ref{lem:renorm} absorbs.  (The package's
market-facing pricer additionally splits tie mass by dead-heat multiplicity
as in \textcite{cotton2021}; the Jacobian theory of this paper applies to
the smooth no-tie map, whose continuum limit has no ties.)

\section{The Hessian-vector product in gory detail}
\label{app:hvp}

Throughout, $v \in \R^n$ is the vector to be multiplied,
$W$ has entries (minimum convention)
\begin{equation}
  w_{ij} = \frac{1}{u}\sum_x f_i(x)\, f_j(x) \prod_{k \ne i,j} S_k(x),
  \label{eq:lattice-w}
\end{equation}
and the goal is $Lv$ with $L = \diag(W\one) - W$, i.e.
$(Lv)_i = \sum_{j\ne i} w_{ij}(v_i - v_j)$.

\subsection{The identity and its cancellation}

Define hazards $h_j(x) = f_j(x)/S_j(x)$ wherever $S_j(x) > 0$.  The key
observation is that the pair mass in \eqref{eq:lattice-w} factors through
the leave-one-out mass of either index:
\[
  f_i f_j \prod_{k\ne i,j} S_k
  = \Bigl( f_i \prod_{k \ne i} S_k \Bigr) \frac{f_j}{S_j}
  = q_i\, h_j
  \qquad (S_j > 0).
\]
Summing over $j \ne i$ with the weights $(v_i - v_j)$,
\[
  u\,(Lv)_i
  = \sum_x \sum_{j \ne i} q_i h_j (v_i - v_j)
  = \sum_x q_i \Bigl( v_i \sum_{j\ne i} h_j - \sum_{j \ne i} h_j v_j \Bigr)
  = \sum_x q_i \bigl( v_i H - G_v \bigr),
\]
where in the last step the sums were completed to run over \emph{all} $j$:
the added $j = i$ term is $q_i h_i (v_i - v_i) = 0$.  This cancellation is
what buys the speedup: $H = \sum_j h_j$ and $G_v = \sum_j h_j v_j$ are
independent of $i$, computed once in $O(nM)$, after which each row is an
inner product of $q_i$ with a shared column vector.  Total: $O(nM)$.  This
is Theorem~\ref{thm:hvp}; it is the differential-level analogue of the
removable-field trick that makes \eqref{eq:lattice-forward} linear in $n$.

Two distinct numerical failures lurk in the division $f_j/S_j$, and they
require different treatment.

\subsection{Failure 1: zero survival with positive mass}

At the top of contestant $j$'s support the clamped CDF reaches $1$ while
$f_j$ is still positive: atoms, distributions truncated at the lattice edge,
and the final support point of any pmf all produce lattice points with
$S_j(x) = 0$, $f_j(x) > 0$.  There $h_j$ does not exist, yet the true pair
mass $f_i f_j \prod_{k \ne i,j} S_k$ is generally \emph{nonzero}: dropping
it (as a naive mask does) makes the fast product disagree with the dense
reference precisely on the degenerate fields one most needs to trust.

\subsection{Failure 2: tiny survival and catastrophic cancellation}

Where $S_j$ is positive but tiny, $h_j = f_j/S_j$ can be enormous.  It then
dominates both $H$ and $G_v$, and the subtraction $v_i H - G_v$---whose
mathematically exact $j$-term is $h_j(v_i - v_j)$, of moderate size---is
computed as a difference of huge floats, losing all the small terms.  With
hazards bounded by $h_{\max}$, the absolute error of a column is bounded by
$C\,n\,h_{\max}\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\,\|v\|_\infty$ for a small
constant $C$; controlling $h_{\max}$ controls the error.

\subsection{The resolution: masked hazards plus exact repairs}

Fix two thresholds, a survival tolerance $\sigma$ (implementation:
$\sigma = 10^{-6}$) and a mass tolerance $\mu$ ($\mu = 10^{-15}$).

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Masked base pass.}  Set $h_j(x) = f_j(x)/S_j(x)$ where
    $S_j(x) \ge \sigma$ and $h_j(x) = 0$ otherwise.  Compute
    $q = f \odot \Lambda$ (prefix--suffix, division-free), $H$, $G_v$, and
    the base row sums $\sum_x q_i (v_i H - G_v)$.  Every hazard is now
    bounded by $h_{\max} \le 1/\sigma = 10^{6}$, so by the bound above the
    cancellation error is
    $\lesssim n\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}/\sigma \approx 10^{-10}\,
    \|v\|_\infty$ per column---negligible against the $10^{-8}$ relative
    test tolerance.  Crucially $G_v$ is accumulated in the same reduction
    order as $H$, so for $v = \one$ the integrand is a bitwise-exact zero:
    the translation gauge $L\one = 0$ holds to the last ulp, not merely to
    rounding.
  \item \textbf{Exact repair pass.}  Masking $h_j$ at a point $x$ deleted
    the pair term $f_i f_j \prod_{k\ne i,j} S_k$ from \emph{every} row
    $i \ne j$ at that point (and only those terms: row $j$'s own
    $(j,i)$-terms flow through $h_i$, which is healthy there).  For each
    masked pair $(j, x)$ with $f_j(x) > \mu$, rebuild the deleted terms
    exactly and division-free: remove row $j$ from the survival column
    $S(\cdot, x)$, compute leave-one-out products of the reduced
    $(n-1)$-vector by prefix--suffix ($O(n)$), multiply by
    $f_i(x)f_j(x)$, and add $(\text{pair mass})\cdot(v_i - v_j)$ into each
    row $i \ne j$.  Batching by $j$---one prefix--suffix call over all of
    contestant $j$'s masked columns at once---replaces thousands of tiny
    array operations by at most $n$ vectorized ones (measured
    $10$--$13\times$ faster than the per-point loop).  No double counting
    occurs: the $(i,j)$ term of row $i$ lives only in $h_j$, so it is
    restored exactly once, by the repair of $(j, x)$; when $i$ and $j$ are
    \emph{both} masked at $x$ (two atoms at one point), row $i$'s term is
    restored by $j$'s repair and row $j$'s by $i$'s.
  \item \textbf{Dropped dust.}  Masked pairs with $f_j(x) \le \mu$ are
    dropped.  Each dropped term is bounded by
    $f_j \cdot 1 \cdot \|v\|_\infty / u \le \mu \|v\|_\infty / u$, and there
    are at most $nM$ of them, so the total omission is
    $\le n M \mu \|v\|_\infty / u \approx 10^{-11}\|v\|_\infty$ at the
    default lattice---again negligible, and \emph{provably} so.
\end{enumerate}

For smooth fields the masked set is a short band at the top of each
support (where $S < \sigma$ but $f > \mu$; for a unit Gaussian at spacing
$0.05$, roughly seventy points per contestant), so the repair pass is a
lower-order cost and the total remains $O(nM + n\cdot\#\text{masked})$.
Because the repair is \emph{exact}, the thresholds trade only speed against
speed: $\sigma$ may be set generously (taming cancellation) without any
accuracy cost, which is why the implementation can afford
$\sigma = 10^{-6}$.

\subsection{Pseudocode}

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.92\linewidth}
\small\ttfamily
\textbf{function} matvec($f$, $S$, $v$):\\
\hspace*{1.5em} $\Lambda \gets$ prefix-suffix leave-one-out products of $S$ \hfill // $O(nM)$\\
\hspace*{1.5em} $q \gets f \odot \Lambda$\\
\hspace*{1.5em} mask $\gets [S < \sigma]$;\quad $h \gets$ where(mask, $0$, $f / S$)\\
\hspace*{1.5em} $H \gets \sum_j h_j$;\quad $G \gets \sum_j h_j v_j$ \hfill // same reduction order\\
\hspace*{1.5em} out$_i \gets \sum_x q_i (v_i H - G)$ \hfill // $O(nM)$\\
\hspace*{1.5em} \textbf{for} each contestant $j$ with masked massy points: \hfill // repairs\\
\hspace*{2.8em} $C \gets$ \{columns $x$: mask$_{j,x}$ and $f_j(x) > \mu$\}\\
\hspace*{2.8em} $\Lambda' \gets$ prefix-suffix products of $S$ with row $j$ deleted, columns $C$ \hfill // $O(n|C|)$\\
\hspace*{2.8em} $m_i \gets \sum_{x \in C} f_i(x) f_j(x) \Lambda'_i(x)$\\
\hspace*{2.8em} out$_i \gets$ out$_i + m_i (v_i - v_j)$ \quad for all $i \ne j$\\
\hspace*{1.5em} \textbf{return} out$/u$
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

An operator form precomputes everything $v$-independent ($\Lambda$, $q$,
$h$, $H$, mask, and all repair masses $m$) once per field; each subsequent
product is then three elementwise passes and the rank-one repair updates,
measured $18\times$ faster than the from-scratch call---the relevant number
inside conjugate gradients, which applies the same field to many vectors.

\subsection{Verification ladder}

The implementation is validated at four independent levels, in increasing
strength: (i) the dense reference \eqref{eq:lattice-w} is computed by a
second, division-free algorithm (per-row leave-one-out over the reduced
field, $O(n^2M)$) and the fast product must match it to $10^{-8}$ relative
on a battery of 24 named fields plus 25 random mixtures of atoms,
near-atoms, truncations, zero-mass sentinels and adversarial two-point
distributions; (ii) $L\one = 0$ must hold bitwise; (iii) central finite
differences of the forward map \eqref{eq:lattice-forward} must converge to
$-L$ at rate $O(u)$ on every smooth field; (iv) the two-Gaussian closed
form must be reproduced at machine precision.  All four hold in the shipped
test battery.

\section{The Newton--CG solver in gory detail}
\label{app:newton}

\textbf{Inputs.}  Unshifted per-contestant base pmfs (heterogeneous shapes
and scales allowed), a strictly positive finite target $\pi \in \R^n_{>0}$,
an optional warm start, tolerance $\tau$ (default $10^{-10}$), iteration
caps.  \textbf{Output.}  Mean-zero abilities $a$, achieved probabilities,
the scale $s$, residual, iteration count, a converged flag, a residual
history, and a stop message.

\paragraph{Residual.}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:renorm} the solver drives
$r(a) = p(a) - s(a)\pi$, $s(a) = \one^\top p(a)/\one^\top\pi$, to zero.
$r$ sums to zero by construction, so no explicit centering or projection is
ever needed, and its Jacobian is exactly $-L$ (minimum convention: $+L$
after the sign dictionary of Appendix~\ref{app:degenerate}); the Newton
equation is $L\delta = r$, $a \gets a + \delta$.

\paragraph{Gauge and the lattice box.}
Abilities are re-centered to mean zero after every accepted step (the gauge
of Theorem~\ref{thm:diffeo}), then clipped componentwise to the
representable box $[-(\ell-2)u, (\ell-2)u]$: beyond it the fractional shift
saturates, the coordinate's column of the Jacobian is identically zero, and
an unclipped iterate would wander meaninglessly.  A converged-or-stalled
solution with some $|a_i|$ \emph{at} the box triggers an explicit
diagnostic---the target requires more ability spread than $\ell u$
affords; enlarge the lattice---rather than a silent stall.  (When the clip
binds, exact zero mean is sacrificed to feasibility; the message says so.)

\paragraph{Inner solve.}
Conjugate gradients on $L\delta = r$, starting from $\delta = 0$.  Every
Krylov vector lies in $\perpone$ automatically ($r$ does, and $L$ maps into
$\perpone$), so the singular direction $\one$ never contaminates the solve.
Each iteration costs one operator product from
Appendix~\ref{app:hvp}; the field structure is precomputed once per Newton
step.  Nonpositive curvature $p^\top L p \le 0$---possible only when the
interaction graph has disconnected (Appendix~\ref{app:degenerate})---
terminates CG with the best iterate.

\paragraph{Globalization.}
The merit function is the squared residual $\|r\|_2^2$ (not the max norm
used for the convergence test): for direction $\delta$ solving
$(L + \lambda I)\delta = r$, $\lambda \ge 0$, the directional derivative of
$\tfrac12\|r\|_2^2$ is $-r^\top L(L+\lambda I)^{-1} r \le 0$, so these are
guaranteed descent directions for the \emph{exact} Jacobian and remain
descent in practice for the $O(u)$-accurate lattice one.  Backtracking
halves the step at most 25 times.  If the pure Newton direction
($\lambda = 0$) fails its line search---which happens when the piecewise
linearity of the lattice map bites, e.g.\ near atoms---the solver retries
with $\lambda \in \{10^{-3}, 10^{-1}, 10\} \times \kappa$, where
$\kappa = r^\top L r / r^\top r$ is the curvature along the residual: a
Levenberg-style continuum from Newton toward scaled gradient descent.  Only
if all of these stall does the solver stop, returning the best iterate,
\texttt{converged = false}, and a message naming the failure.

\paragraph{Stopping and diagnostics.}
Success is $\|r\|_\infty < \tau$.  The message distinguishes: converged;
Newton direction vanished (interaction graph disconnected---zero-mass,
dominated, or off-lattice contestant); line search stalled despite
regularization; iteration cap reached; and, orthogonally, whether the
solution is pinned at the lattice box.  The residual history (initial plus
after each accepted step) is returned for convergence inspection; the
history in \S\ref{sec:experiments} is typical.

\paragraph{Complexity.}
Per Newton step: one $O(nM)$ operator build, $k_{\mathrm{cg}}$ products at
$O(nM)$ each (tens suffice; the spectrum of $L$ is clustered for
well-mixed fields), and one forward evaluation per line-search probe.  With
$k_N$ Newton steps the total is
$O\bigl(k_N (k_{\mathrm{cg}} + \text{probes})\, nM\bigr)$, near-linear in
the field size and, per Table~\ref{tab:timing}, cheaper end-to-end than
assembling a single dense Jacobian for $n \gtrsim 100$.

\section{Degenerate fields, exact semantics, and the sign dictionary}
\label{app:degenerate}

\paragraph{Sign dictionary.}
The main text uses maximum-wins with
$p_i = \int f_i \prod_{k \ne i} F_k$ and $DT = +L(w)$,
$w_{ij} = \int f_i f_j \prod_{k\ne i,j} F_k$.  The implementation uses
minimum-wins (race times) with survivals in place of CDFs and
$Dp = -L(w)$, $w_{ij} = \frac1u\sum f_i f_j \prod_{k \ne i,j} S_k$.  The
two are conjugate under $x \mapsto -x$, $a \mapsto -a$, which swaps $F$ and
$S$ and reverses the orientation of ``better''; $W$ maps to $W$ and only
the sign of the Jacobian flips.

\paragraph{Zero-mass contestants.}
A pmf of total mass zero is the package's sentinel for a contestant shifted
entirely off the lattice.  It has $S \equiv 1$, $f \equiv 0$: it never
finishes, wins with probability zero, leaves every other pair weight
untouched, and contributes a zero row and column to $W$.  $L$ gains a
second null vector; $\lambda_2 = 0$.

\paragraph{Deterministic domination.}
An atom that some other contestant always beats has its win probability
frozen at a boundary face.  \emph{Every} weight incident to it vanishes:
each pair mass at its atom's location carries the dominating contestant's
survival factor, which is zero there.  The dominated contestant is an
isolated vertex---the graph disconnects exactly as for zero mass, by a
different mechanism.  Both cases make the affected coordinate
unidentifiable from winning probabilities; the solver of
Appendix~\ref{app:newton} detects the vanished Newton direction and says
so, and callers should drop such contestants.

\paragraph{Atomic ties.}
Two identical atoms always tie.  On the lattice their mutual weight is
$w = 1/u$ (the empty product over third parties is $1$), diverging as
$u \to 0$: the discrete signature of the continuum map's genuine
non-differentiability at an atomic tie, where an infinitesimal shift flips
the outright winner discontinuously.  A third coincident atom sends every
pair mass to zero (some third-party survival vanishes at the atom), so the
trio's weight matrix is identically zero.  These are not pathologies of the
algorithm but exact features of the map, and the fast product reproduces
the dense reference in both cases through the repair pass of
Appendix~\ref{app:hvp}.

\paragraph{Adversarial near-zero survivals.}
A two-point pmf with mass $1 - \epsilon$ then $\epsilon$ has survival
$\epsilon$ over the gap.  For $\epsilon < \sigma$ the huge hazard is masked
and repaired exactly; for $\epsilon$ just above $\sigma$ the hazard
$\approx 1/\epsilon$ is retained and the cancellation bound of
Appendix~\ref{app:hvp} caps the damage at
$\sim n\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}/\sigma$.  Both regimes are exercised in the
test battery at $\epsilon = 10^{-7}$ and $10^{-5}$.
