Tournament designer

A tournament is an estimator: nature fixes a ranking, each match is a noisy pairwise comparison, and the format is a rule for turning a budget of comparisons into a guess at “who is best.” This page runs the same Thurstone Case V model as the rest of the site — performance = ability + Gaussian noise, highest performance wins, Elo-calibrated so a +100 rating edge is about a 63% single-game chance — to ask which 48-team World Cup format most reliably crowns the truly best team, for the same calendar length.

Tune the rules, draw a bracket to play one season, then simulate to Monte-Carlo the format against the real World Cup and a pure Swiss league — all in your browser.

What the model is telling you

Across thousands of Monte-Carlo seasons, every Swiss-style format beats the group-of-4-plus-knockout World Cup by a wide margin. The lever is not eliminating teams on thin evidence: the real World Cup sends the true best team home before the final about two-thirds of the time, on a single knockout upset or a three-game group. You can still send teams home for drama — just cut on an accumulated record, protect the top two into the final (the “most-plays-least” bracket), and let the semis be Record defends so a season of results is not coin-flipped away. Team names here are illustrative; abilities are re-drawn at random each season.
